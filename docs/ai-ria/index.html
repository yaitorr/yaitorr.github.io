AI Risk and Impact Assessment (AI-RIA) Proposal

A Pilot Framework for Colleague.ai in Fairfax County Public Schools

Executive Summary

Generative AI (GenAI) has the potential to unlock 21st century education. Teachers using tools like ChatGPT self-report significant time savings (Gallup, Inc., 2025), freeing time to reinvest in their classroom (higher-quality instruction and educational experiences), while avoiding overtime. However, given the risks of general-purpose GenAI (Qadir, 2023), and harms already being reported (Merod, 2026), FCPS must opt to deploy purpose-built educational technology such as Colleague.ai at a measured pace. It is their duty to develop a human-centered pilot program, calibrated by the proposed AI Risk and Impact Assessment (AI-RIA). This approach will protect the personal freedoms of all its participants, help regain the community's trust in edtech, and, more importantly, ensure the safety and well-being of children.

Product Overview: AI as Educational Infrastructure

Colleague.ai pairs teacher-facing instructional tools (lesson planning, grading, IEP and multilingual learner support) with a purpose-built tutoring layer for students and acceptance and alignment analytics for school and district leadership. Its primary goal is to leverage generative AI models trained by researchers, classroom teachers, and experts on instruction to augment teacher performance at scale, increasing teachers' effectiveness in the classroom while removing the institutional friction common to large, diverse organizations such as the Fairfax County Public School system (FCPS). In doing so, it is meant to become critical educational infrastructure, enabling a seamless flow of information across organizational tiers (student, teacher, admin, leadership) and maintaining alignment with FCPS's current priorities.

Justification for AI Risk and Impact Assessment

Colleague.ai's design is backed by research on teacher-driven, instructional design methodology (Sarkar et al., 2025), and continues evolving through federally funded research (Institute of Education Sciences, 2024). However, the myriad detrimental effects to a person's cognitive skills (Shen & Tamkin, 2026), and minorities' freedom of expression (Miao & Holmes, 2023) — in addition to the tool's potential for student-driven misuse, and academic gaslighting via its hallucinatory tendencies (Maiberg, 2026; Qadir, 2023) — makes it imperative for FCPS to engage in a comprehensive AI Risk and Impact Assessment (AI-RIA). Not just to assuage the increasing community revolt against educational tech (Kingkade, 2026), but also to present teachers and other stakeholders with evidence-based information on the well-substantiated risks and benefits of the technology, as it relates to the broad and culturally diverse population they serve (Fairfax County Public Schools, n.d.), and their own personal freedoms.

AI Lifecycle Stage

Colleague.ai's own development is centered on Washington state school districts, where it is currently undergoing verification and validation through IES-funded research (Institute of Education Sciences, 2024). FCPS, however, has not yet adopted the tool, and their governance of GenAI tooling is only now catching up: the Board and administration are moving towards effective AI policy (Stout, 2026), including a multilateral Educational Technology Review Committee (ETRC) tasked with "evaluating, piloting, scaling, and sunsetting educational technology tools..." (Frisch & Dannan, 2026, p. 5). This places FCPS in their own, distinct verification and validation stage of the AI lifecycle (Mäntymäki et al., 2022, p. 9) — one informed by a controlled pilot, conducted under IES standards (REL Appalachia at SRI International, 2021), that the AI-RIA itself directly shapes, confirming whether Colleague.ai's results in Washington hold for FCPS's own student population before any district-wide deployment.

Recommended Impact Assessment Framework

FCPS must pursue a hybrid assessment, anchored in the NIST AI RMF (National Institute of Standards and Technology [NIST], 2023) and executed through the practical, domain-aligned guidance of the EdTech Evolved AI Impact Assessment Template (Jennings, 2024). This approach will allow FCPS to categorize risks of harm to people, the organization, and the learning environment identified by the NIST AI RMF, through a series of calibrated questions.

IES Pilot Lifecycle and AI-RIA Mappings

IES's seven-phase pilot model (REL Appalachia at SRI International, 2021) maps cleanly onto AI-RIA inputs, as shown below:

Pilot Lifecycle Phase AI-RIA Inputs
Roles and responsibilities Help FCPS identify and engage relevant AI actors (domain experts, community members, etc.) based on risk and potential for impact, negative or positive.
Study questions and planning Prioritize AI trustworthiness measures (safety, fairness, and transparency) over shallow productivity metrics.
Sample identification and recruiting Secure parental consent and cultivate trust; recruit a diverse population sample (Title I, MLLs, etc.), and protect participants from known risks of bias and threats to personal freedoms.
Data collection and analysis Comply with federal and state law, require both quantitative and qualitative metrics, and set post-deployment monitoring.
Conclusion and next steps Provide a nuanced view on results and recorded behaviors; foster multilateral discussions and decision-making; drive continued improvement if scaling is decided.

Additional Standards, Law and Regulations

Colleague.ai is already compliant with GDPR, COPPA/FERPA, and SOC2 (Colleague.ai, 2026). While this demonstrates accountability, FCPS should treat it as a floor, not a ceiling — GenAI's rapid evolution continuously introduces novel risks. Two additional resources are critical during the pilot:

  • UNESCO's Guidance for generative AI in education and research (Miao & Holmes, 2023): Grounds AI RMF actors in an education context and equips FCPS with internationally recognized policy options for governing the pilot and beyond.
  • GUARD Act: Bipartisan legislation pending Senate floor action that would ban AI companions from interacting with minors due to mental health risks (Merod, 2026). FCPS should verify Colleague.ai's tutoring chatbot complies before scaling.

Supporting AI Actors

Following NIST's AI RMF, and Miao & Holmes GenAI guidelines for greater domain fidelity, we can identify three main actors:

AI Actor Main Function Role
FCPS Admin and Board Govern Finalize FCPS AI policy, identify community priorities in collaboration with the ETRC, secure community buy-in and parental consent.
School administrators Operations Provide teacher training, liaise with vendors and parents, validate pedagogical and district-priority alignment through collected metrics, and keep humans in the loop.
Teachers Test, evaluation, verification, and validation Validate benefits of AI-augmented lesson plans and grading support, promote AI literacy among students and parents; monitor risk and impact to students and the classroom environment.

Conclusions and Business Value

Colleague.ai's "AI as education infrastructure" approach to GenAI can unlock benefits for FCPS at cost and scale. It has the potential to reduce institutional friction and augment teachers' capacity to serve MLL students, freeing up time for higher-quality instruction overall. This could ultimately allow teachers to experiment with novel, civic-oriented learning experiences — community-project-based learning, learning by teaching the chatbot, policy brief synthesis, civic hackathons, and more.

However, FCPS must go beyond potential benefits and hype to diligently assess and validate the tool. The proposed hybrid AI-RIA, paired with a human-centered pilot, lets the district meet that duty: it provides the transparency and accountability controls needed to protect personal freedoms, while creating space for the constructive community dialogue FCPS currently lacks. Taken together, these are measured steps toward renovating community trust and unlocking AI-augmented education for the 21st century.
